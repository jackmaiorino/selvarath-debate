## Context

The project studies AI debate as an oversight protocol: an honest and a dishonest debater argue a question about
a fictional world document; a judge that cannot see the document picks an answer, in some conditions after asking
a limited number of oracle-verified factual queries. The preregistered final experiment ("main") is closed and its
analysis outputs are frozen. Two people are now writing a paper-style final report. One of them (with Claude)
writes the methods, results, figures and supplement; the other author writes the abstract, introduction,
background and discussion later.

A stated requirement from the co-author: the main text must be comfortably readable and must not read like a
statistics paper. Plain rates, differences and 95% intervals in the main text; the multiplicity procedure,
bootstrap details, sensitivity analyses and audit bounds belong in the supplement. Please do not ask for more
statistical machinery in the main text. Do flag any place where the plain wording says something the frozen
results do not support, or hides a caveat a careful reader needs.

## Questions

1. Numbers. Is every number in the four .tex files and the generated tables correct against the sources, with the
   right unit, sign, denominator and judge panel? Check in particular the seven-judge pooled rates (derived in the
   scripts as (8p - 1)/7 from the eight-judge pooled curve, estimates and interval endpoints alike), the hard-coded
   numbers in `appendix.tex` and `methods.tex` (they are typed by hand, not generated), and the cost figures.
2. Plain-language statistics. Each of these paraphrases must be faithful to the registered analysis; say if any
   overstates or misstates it, and give replacement wording at the same reading level:
   - "corrected p" for the Holm-adjusted intersection-union p-value;
   - "the registered test supports a peak at one query";
   - "The data rule out a peak that large: allowing for the several comparisons made, the differences at one
     query are at most 1.19 and 1.24 points";
   - "Six queries were no better than none";
   - the description of the gate worst-case analysis in Section 4.4 and Appendix D ("assume that every affected
     session counts against the finding", "would lose its statistical support if 0.25% of sessions were affected");
   - "In most of those sessions the flags concern the form of the query" (check this against the audit's
     definitions of exposed and leak-exposed);
   - the exploratory section 4.5, including "The difference, 115 pairs, is exactly the 0.77-point rise".
3. Methods. Does Section 3 describe what was actually run: benchmark authoring and the retention rule (as amended,
   if any amendment changed it), the split, the debates and the choice of pairing, the conditions, the oracle, the
   gate and its state machine, scoring and weights? Is anything materially wrong or missing that a reader would
   need to interpret the results?
4. Deviations. Is Appendix B's account of the deviations and of the development runs accurate and complete enough?
   Is the required disclosure about the confirmation run's amended retry allowance correctly stated?
5. Figures. From `make_figures.py` and the captions: does any figure encode something misleading (axes, the
   treatment of the unbounded interval for Opus at budget 0, the seven-judge derivation, the shares in the
   explanation figure, where the "unparsed" share uses the census count 6 of 331)?
6. Omissions. Is any result or caveat from the reviewed 10/29 report missing from the main text where a careful
   reader would need it (not merely nice to have)?
