POSITION: MAINTAIN. The disclosure is warranted, but the proposed matched-session check would not settle performance on wrong choices under a larger output allowance.

ARGUMENT: The latest argument substitutes a six-query outcome for an unobserved no-query outcome. Those are different treatments. Consequently, “fewer than 6 wrong would mean wrong choices did not fall” does not follow.

A concrete counterexample using your counts: suppose six-query judgments were wrong on only four of the 19 sessions truncated without queries. Suppose ten of those truncated no-query calls would have answered wrongly if allowed to finish, while the single truncated six-query call would have answered correctly. Wrong-choice totals would then be 38 + 10 = 48 without queries and 43 + 0 = 43 with six. Wrong choices would fall despite your proposed check finding fewer than six wrong.

Likewise, the 15 correct one-query outcomes among 24 unreadable no-query cases cannot estimate how those no-query calls would have finished. Verification might have changed their answers, which is precisely the possibility under investigation. Comparing wrong choices among readable responses also conditions on completion, whose frequency changes across conditions.

The additional query calls and different reasoning lengths strengthen the case for explaining the protocol’s unequal opportunities to produce a verdict. They do not resolve the missing answers.

IF CONCEDING: Not applicable. I accept the narrowed recommendation to disclose failures and distinguish scored errors from incorrect readable choices.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT: Frozen records can establish the exact decomposition into incorrect readable verdicts and unsuccessful verdict production. They cannot identify answers truncated calls would have produced with more capacity without additional assumptions or new data. Within this closed study, disclose that limitation and retain the registered composite result without assigning a direction to the unobserved counterfactual.
