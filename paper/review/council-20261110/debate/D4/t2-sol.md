POSITION: MAINTAIN. The narrowed disclosure is justified, but the proposed matched-session check cannot settle counterfactual wrong-answer accuracy.

ARGUMENT: The new “what would settle it” substitutes a six-query verdict for an unobserved no-query verdict. Those are different treatments, precisely the difference being studied. Even if all 19 corresponding six-query verdicts were correct, the truncated no-query continuations could have been correct or wrong. Both possibilities fit that observation and imply different directions for the hypothetical accuracy contrast.

Likewise, the one-query outcomes corresponding to unreadable no-query replies do not estimate how those replies would have ended without queries. The threshold calculations give necessary conditions on unknown completions; they supply no evidence that those conditions hold. “Worse than guessing” adds an unjustified reference probability for this selected group of long replies.

If the proposed check instead concerns pairs readable in both conditions, say that explicitly. It can describe that selected subset, but readability can itself depend on condition, so it does not recover the full-panel wrong-answer contrast. This leaves a useful, defensible conclusion: disclose the unequal failure counts and distinguish the registered composite from accuracy among completed verdicts.

IF CONCEDING: Not applicable. I accept the withdrawal of the two original statements.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT: The scoring rule and counts settle the registered composite. Counterfactual accuracy under a larger output allowance requires additional data under a specified continuation protocol, outside this frozen study. The report should disclose that gap rather than impute truncated answers from query-condition verdicts.
