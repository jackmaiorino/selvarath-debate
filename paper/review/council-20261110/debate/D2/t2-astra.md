POSITION: MAINTAIN (Shared model identity warrants disclosure of possible correlated errors; it does not establish the direction of bias in the reported attribution percentages.)

ARGUMENT: Your equation establishes a conditional result, but you have promoted the possibility being disputed into its premise. “A plausible common-error threat” does not assert positive class-conditional covariance. Moreover, your comparison requires fixing the full class-conditional response distributions, not merely auditor accuracy: equally accurate three-class auditors can distribute their wrong answers differently.

There is a further gap between increased agreement and inflated “judge misread” labels. Your equation concerns agreement between two categorical answers. The labellers instead saw the oracle answer, world, and judge explanation and applied an attribution rubric. The relevant error is accepting an incorrect oracle answer **within the selected harmful flips**. Generic response covariance does not establish that conditional error rate. Nor must accepting the oracle’s answer transfer a case into “judge misread”: the rubric also permits peripheral, unused, overridden, and unclear labels.

Your claim that confident shared blind spots escaped the 21-case review is likewise a possible failure mode, not evidence about its selection rule or omitted cases.

I support replacing the declarative headline and naming the reviewer. Appropriate disclosure is: “By the Opus 5.5 labellers’ assessment, most sampled harmful flips involved a correct oracle answer followed by an unsupported conclusion. Because the oracle and primary labellers used the same model, shared interpretive errors could go undetected; the reported intervals exclude labelling uncertainty.” This communicates the specific risk without asserting that the 74% estimate is inflated or that another equally accurate model would reduce it.
