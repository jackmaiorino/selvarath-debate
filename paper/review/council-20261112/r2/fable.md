MISSED BY ALL:
1. Qwen3.8's cut-off query steps. The draft counts only cut-off verdicts for Qwen3.8 (19, 6, 2 and 1 with none, one, two and six queries). The frozen readout (paper/data/main_readout.json) shows that 11, 10 and 6 of its query-step replies were also cut off at the limit with one, two and six queries. By the code (finalphase/judging.py, lines 95 to 101) a cut-off query step ends the querying and the judge is then asked for its verdict in a new call; it is not scored as an error. So Qwen3.8 had 19 cut-off replies with no queries and 17 with one, but only 6 of the 17 counted as errors. The imbalance that 3.5, 4.3 and Appendix A report is therefore partly a product of the scoring: a reply that runs out of room costs the verdict when no queries are offered and usually only the queries when they are. This cannot produce the rise at one query (it adds errors on the no-query side), and it bears on Qwen3.8's place below its no-query level and on the 0.05-point pooled difference at six. It assigns no direction to what the replies would have said and needs no new analysis. Wording for Appendix A: "Qwen3.8's query-step replies were also cut off at the limit (11, 10 and 6 with one, two and six queries). A cut-off query step ends the querying and the judge is then asked for its verdict, so it is not scored as an error. A reply that runs out of room therefore costs the verdict when no queries are offered and usually only the queries when they are." In 4.3, after "against 1 with six queries", add: "; with queries on offer its cut-offs fell mostly on query steps, which are not scored".
