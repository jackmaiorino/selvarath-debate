The proposed bullet’s final clause also extends beyond the decomposition. Within this dataset, reweighting two subsets with different observed effects necessarily changes the pooled estimate. That does not establish that the same relationship holds across benchmarks, whose flagged questions, baseline difficulty and effects could differ.

I would replace the ending with: “These questions account for 53 of the 115 net harmful flips despite comprising 24.5% of analysed questions. This is a descriptive concentration within this benchmark; whether it generalises is unknown.” With that wording, I support including the bullet. The arithmetic needs neither a new test nor a causal interpretation.
