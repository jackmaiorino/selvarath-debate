## The dispute

Finding 1 of Member E (severity given: major), the part about how to describe the 262 questions that drew two or more validator flags.

Defender(s): Member E (author(s) of the finding). Challenger(s): Member A, Member B, Member C, Member D.

What is in dispute: NOT the arithmetic (53 of the 115 net harmful flips fall on the 262 flagged questions) and NOT whether the validator flags should be defined in the text: the members agree on both, and Member E has narrowed the main-text part to a definition, with the arithmetic in Appendix A. In dispute are the label and the inference in the proposed new limitation: calling these questions "contested answer keys", saying "the answer key is contested on a quarter of the analysed questions", and the closing clause "so part of what is scored as error may be a defensible alternative reading". The four challengers say this overstates what a flag establishes.

## The finding as written in round 1 (Member E)

1. Major. The 806-question check is reported in a way that hides where the rise sits, and contested answer keys appear nowhere as a limitation. Section 4.6 says: "On the 806 questions that drew at most one validator flag for a possible alternative reading, the rise at one query is smaller (0.55 against 0.77 points) and still supported". A reader sees "robust on clean questions". But the pooled contrast is a question-weighted mean with no missing cells, so the remainder follows by arithmetic on frozen values. In main_analysis.json, determinacy_0_or_1, D10 = 0.0048077 on the eight-judge scale, which is 0.5495 on the seven-judge scale and exactly 62 net harmful flips over 806 x 14 pairs. So 53 of the 115 net harmful flips (46%) come from the 262 questions (24.5%) where two or more of the four validator answers recorded a reading of the document that could reverse the answer. That is about 1.44 points on those questions against 0.55 on the rest. (By the same arithmetic the one-against-six leg is about 1.15 points there.) Appendix A gives the flag distribution (84 questions flagged by all four answers) but draws no conclusion, and Appendix I's 14 limits never mention that the answer key is contested on a quarter of the analysed questions. This changes how a reader should take "error" and the explanation patterns in 4.5: on these questions a "wrong" verdict after an oracle answer may sometimes be a defensible reading. It also cuts the other way: flagged questions are probably where debate judges err most (the world-versus-six gap there is about 3.8 points), so absolute concentration partly reflects room to move. Either way it belongs in the text. Resolve: (a) In 4.6, replace the sentence with: "On the 806 questions that at most one of the four validator answers flagged as open to another reading, the rise at one query is smaller (0.55 against 0.77 points) and still supported, and a peak at two queries is also supported. So nearly half of the net rise (53 of the 115 net harmful flips, Section 4.5) comes from the other 262 questions." To make room, cut "By the registered rule these checks can neither rescue nor overturn the main result (Appendix A)" down to "(Appendix A)". (b) Add a bullet to Appendix I: "Contested answer keys. In 262 of the 1,068 analysed questions, at least two of the four validator answers recorded another reading of the document that could reverse the answer (all four in 84). These questions were kept by design. The one-query rise is still supported without them, but nearly half of the net rise comes from them, so part of what is scored as error may be a defensible alternative reading." (c) In Appendix A's validator-flag paragraph, add the 53-of-115 arithmetic and say that it is derived from two registered estimates and is not a separate test.

## The defender's own round-2 remarks on this finding (Member E)

WITHDRAWN: none. I narrow finding 1(a). In 4.6 the main text should carry only the definition of the flags and the fact that the rise concentrates there, without the 53-of-115 count. The arithmetic goes to Appendix A, labelled as derived from two registered estimates. This answers the forking-paths concern in my own counterargument and saves main-text space.

## Opening refutation(s) from round 2

Member A:

Member E, finding 1(a): AGREE: The subset arithmetic is a legitimate descriptive decomposition, and residual answer-key uncertainty deserves acknowledgment. It does not require a new hypothesis test, but it is optional supplementary context rather than a necessary main-text statistic.
Member E, finding 1(b): REFUTE: “Contested answer keys on a quarter of questions” overstates these flags. The validator prompt explicitly permits filling gaps with assumptions not ruled out by the document, while retained responses must still select the key and reject equal defensibility of the alternative; flags therefore do not establish that scored errors were reasonable answers.

Member B:

Member E, finding 1(a): AGREE: (Where the rise sits: 53 of the 115 net flips on the 262 flagged questions; define the flags.) State it once, in 4.6 or Appendix A, as arithmetic on two registered estimates.
Member E, finding 1(b): REFUTE: (The label "contested answer keys" and "may be a defensible alternative reading".) The validator prompt (finalphase/prompts_authoring.py) told validators to "try hard to construct" a reversing reading from any assumption the document does not rule out, and asked separately whether the other answer was about equally defensible; on every kept question all four responses chose the key and none said yes to the second. Title the bullet "Questions flagged for another reading" and state those two facts in place of the last clause.

Member C:

Member E, finding 1(a): AGREE: The alternative-reading flags deserve an explicit limitation and a clear definition. The persistence of the rise on the restricted subset does not eliminate uncertainty about interpretation of retained flagged questions.
Member E, finding 1(b): REFUTE: “Contested answer keys” overstates what the flags establish. The validator prompt separately permitted gap-filling assumptions when seeking reversing readings, while every retained response selected the key and rejected the alternative as equally defensible under a reasonable reading.
Member E, finding 1(c): AGREE: The 53-of-115 decomposition follows descriptively from the registered totals and subset result. It can be reported in the supplement, but it neither proves key errors nor requires a new main-text subgroup narrative.

Member D:

  - *Calibrate **E1** ("contested answer keys" as a Major limitation):* **E1**'s exact subtraction on frozen outputs (`53` of `115` net harmful flips, `~1.44` points, sit in the `262` questions with 2–4 validator alternative-reading flags) is illuminating and belongs alongside defining those flags in Section 3.1 (joining **B5** and **D4.1**). However, calling those 262 items *"contested answer keys"* where wrong debate verdicts may be *"a defensible alternative reading"* is overstated: all four blind validator passes matched the key on every kept question, and judges reading the document directly without debate erred only **`0.59%`** overall (`0.00%–0.80%` outside Haiku). Those 262 questions are better described as items offering **textual hooks for adversarial framing** (hence higher debate error and larger point swings), not defective keys—and on the 806 clean questions, peaks at *both* 1 and 2 queries are supported.
