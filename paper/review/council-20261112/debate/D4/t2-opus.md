ARGUMENT: There is new evidence on size, from paper/data/main_readout.json. Haiku's own writing before the verdict in query conditions was small. At one query, the single query step averaged 91 output tokens (median 39, p90 294). At two queries, each step averaged 55 (median 34). At six queries, each step averaged 42 (median 30), over about 5.6 steps. So at one query, the condition behind all 331 harmful flips and the 0.77-point rise, the typical Haiku session wrote one bare CLAIM line and then got the oracle's answer before its verdict. Its verdict turn also shrank from 777 to 468 tokens. This cuts both ways. The deliberation confound is real and worth disclosing, and its likeliest reach is the six-query decline (3.84 of 5.72 points), where the extra writing builds up. For 4.5, though, D's phrase "before any chain-of-thought" says nothing that separates the harmful-flip verdicts from the no-query ones. The faithfulness caveat already in Appendix E ("may not be what drove the verdicts") is the part the main text lacks. Proposed 4.5 wording, which keeps A's point: "They rest on the judges' own accounts, which the format asked for after the verdict line and which may not be what drove the verdicts; Haiku, with no hidden reasoning, supplied about half of all harmful flips, and only harmful flips were read; ..." Proposed 4.3 wording, within-judge per B: "Haiku ran without hidden reasoning in a format that asks for the verdict first, so each query step also gave it room to write before its verdict; the design cannot separate that from verification." I accept D's point 2 only in its own parenthetical form: no prior trace limits attribution and does not prove unfaithfulness.
