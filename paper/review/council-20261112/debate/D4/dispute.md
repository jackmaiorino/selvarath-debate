## The dispute

Finding 1 of Member D (severity given: major): the verdict format asks for the verdict before the reasoning, and Claude Haiku 4.5 was the one judge run without extended thinking.

Defender(s): Member D (author(s) of the finding). Challenger(s): Member A, Member B, Member C, Member E.

What is in dispute: NOT whether the paper should disclose that Haiku ran without extended thinking and that each query step gave it more room to write before its verdict, which the design cannot separate from verification: all five members agree on that. In dispute are the stronger statements in the finding and the wording built on them: (i) that with no queries Haiku committed to its verdict "in a single forward pass" with "zero pre-verdict reasoning"; (ii) that Haiku's written reasons are "structurally post-hoc generation", and the proposed replacement in 4.5 ("rest on explanations written after the verdict line, with no prior thinking tokens for Haiku, which supplied half of all harmful flips"), which also removes "only harmful flips were read"; (iii) the proposed 4.3 sentence that the format "requir[es] the verdict before the written reasoning, confounding model scale with pre-verdict deliberation"; and (iv) that Qwen3.8's cut-offs reflect long reasoning on unresolved debates.

## The finding as written in round 1 (Member D)

1. **Major — Unacknowledged design confound: `VERDICT` precedes `REASONING` while the dominant judge (Claude Haiku 4.5) had extended thinking disabled.**
   - **Failure / evidence:** In Appendix F (p. 19), the verdict prompt requires line 1 to be `VERDICT: [Position A / Position B]` *before* `CONFIDENCE:` and `REASONING:`. Six of the seven judges ran with extended thinking / reasoning tokens before visible output (`medium effort` with 16,000-token limits for GPT-5.6 Luna/Terra/Sol, Sonnet 5.5, and Opus 5.5; provider default with a 16,384-token limit for Qwen3.8). **Claude Haiku 4.5**—already the weakest model—was the *only* judge run with **`no extended thinking`** and a **2,048-token limit** (Section 3.3; Table 15). That creates three unacknowledged consequences across the paper:
     1. *Baseline error and the 6-query recovery (Section 4.3):* At 0 queries, Haiku had to commit to its verdict token in a single forward pass over a ~3,800-word debate with **zero pre-verdict reasoning tokens**, whereas in multi-query conditions it generated up to six intermediate query turns (producing malformed free-text steps in 29–36% of sessions, Table 9, and getting rule-breaking queries admitted in 42.5% and 57.5% of audited 2- and 6-query sessions, Appendix D) before emitting `VERDICT:`. Haiku alone supplies **70%** of pooled no-query error (21.21% vs. 0.23–2.86%) and **67%** of the pooled drop from 1 to 6 queries (3.84 of 5.72 total percentage points across the seven judges; Haiku and Qwen3.8 together supply **97%**). Across the other six judges, mean error moves from 1.53% (0 queries) to 2.20% (1 query) and stays elevated at 1.89% (6 queries).
     2. *Exploratory reading of `REASONING:` (Section 4.5, Appendix E):* Because `REASONING:` is generated *after* `VERDICT:`, for non-thinking Haiku—which supplies **170 of the 331 harmful flips (51%)**—the written explanation is structurally post-hoc generation conditioned on an already-sampled verdict token, not reasoning that preceded the choice.
     3. *Qwen3.8's cut-off asymmetry (Section 3.5, Appendix A):* Conversely, Qwen3.8 exhausting a 16,384-token limit 19 times at 0 queries vs. once at 6 queries is consistent with long pre-verdict thinking chains over unresolved debates when no queries were offered.
   - **Resolution:** Disclose the `VERDICT`-before-`REASONING` ordering and its interaction with Haiku's `no extended thinking` setting in Sections 4.3, 4.5, and Appendix I (and Appendix E "Cautions"). In Section 4.3 (end of para 1), add one sentence (~28 words, paid for by the 58-word tightening of Section 4.4 in Finding 8): *"Haiku was also the only judge run without extended thinking (with a 2,048-token cap) in a format requiring the verdict before the written reasoning, confounding model scale with pre-verdict deliberation (Appendices F and G)."* In Section 4.5 (last sentence), change *"rest on the judges' own accounts of their reasoning; only harmful flips were read, and Haiku supplied about half of all harmful flips"* to *"rest on explanations written after the verdict line—with no prior thinking tokens for Haiku, which supplied half of all harmful flips"*.

## The defender's own round-2 remarks on this finding (Member D)

  2. *Refine **D1** on Qwen3.8:* State strictly what Table 15 establishes—Haiku 4.5 was the *only* judge explicitly configured with `no extended thinking` (`2,048`-token cap), against `medium effort` (`16,000` tokens) on five frontier judges and `provider default` (`16,384` tokens) on Qwen3.8—without assuming Qwen3.8's internal thinking mode beyond the text.
     2. *Deliberation and post-hoc explanation confound:* Appendix F (p. 19) requires line 1 of the verdict turn to be `VERDICT:` *before* `CONFIDENCE:` and `REASONING:`. Haiku 4.5 was the *only* judge run with `no extended thinking` and a `2,048`-token cap (Table 15). At 0 queries Haiku emitted `VERDICT:` with zero pre-verdict reasoning tokens, whereas multi-query budgets gave it up to six prior turns (malformed free-text in 29–36% of sessions, Table 9; shrinking its verdict turn from 777 to 468 mean tokens per `main_readout.json`), confounding verification with extra pre-verdict deliberation on 67% of the 1-to-6 drop. In Section 4.5, Haiku supplies **170 of 331 harmful flips (51%)**, and its `REASONING:` text was generated *after* the verdict token with no prior thinking tokens.

## Opening refutation(s) from round 2

Member A:

Member D, finding 1(a): AGREE: Verdict-first prompting and Haiku’s lack of extended thinking make additional query turns a plausible source of extra deliberation. A concise supplementary explanation would sharpen an already acknowledged limitation.
Member D, finding 1(b): REFUTE: “Single forward pass” and “zero pre-verdict deliberation” are stronger than the prompt and settings establish. Absence of explicit thinking tokens does not mean absence of computation or reasoning, and the requested output order does not establish every response’s actual order.
Member D, finding 1(c): REFUTE: Explanations written after a verdict cannot have caused that earlier token, but they are not thereby proven to be unfaithful rationalizations. The proposed replacement also removes the important warning that only harmful flips were read.
Member D, finding 1(d): UNSURE: Longer unresolved reasoning is a possible explanation of Qwen’s cut-offs, but the counts do not establish it. It should not become an asserted explanation without evidence beyond the reported totals.

Member B:

Member D, finding 1(a): AGREE: (Query steps give Haiku extra room before its verdict; Haiku's shares of the error and of the fall.) Same as my finding 4; I checked the 2,048-token limit and zero reasoning tokens (preregistration section 4; paper/data/main_readout.json), and 70% of no-query error and 3.84 of 5.72 judge-points of the fall reproduce from Table 8.
Member D, finding 1(b): REFUTE: (Haiku's explanations are "structurally post-hoc"; the proposed 4.5 wording.) Not established: the format asks for the verdict first, but the parser (parse_verdict_strict) takes a VERDICT line anywhere in the reply, compliance was never measured, and Haiku's no-query verdict replies average 777 tokens. Write "the required format puts the verdict before the reasons" and no more.
Member D, finding 1(c): AGREE: (Qwen3.8's cut-offs reflect long reasoning on its first reply.) main_readout.json supports it: Qwen3.8's reasoning averages 2,532 tokens on a no-query verdict and 3,559 on a one-query query step, and 11, 10 and 6 of its query-step replies were also cut off (MISSED BY ALL 1).
Member D, finding 1(d): REFUTE: (Proposed 4.3 clause "confounding model scale with pre-verdict deliberation".) That clause explains Haiku's error level by a setting the design never varied, the gloss four seats rejected in the first council ("settings as explanations"). The supportable sentence is within-judge: each query step also gave Haiku room to write before its verdict, and the design cannot separate that from verification.

Member C:

Member D, finding 1(a): AGREE: The interaction between verdict-first formatting, disabled extended thinking and additional query turns deserves an explicit scope caveat. It limits attribution to verification alone; it does not invalidate comparison of the complete offered-budget protocols.
Member D, finding 1(b): REFUTE: Explanations generated after a verdict are conditioned on that verdict, but their order does not establish that they are unfaithful rationalizations. Describe their placement and uncertain faithfulness without claiming that Haiku performed no internal reasoning.
Member D, finding 1(c): REFUTE: Unequal Qwen cut-offs are documented, but the counts do not diagnose unresolved debates or long thinking chains as their cause. That mechanistic interpretation should not become a required factual disclosure.

Member E:

Member D, finding 1(a): AGREE (as a disclosure only): Haiku had no hidden reasoning, and query steps gave it extra generation (confirmed in main_readout.json, see B4). REFUTE the stronger premise that Haiku had to commit its verdict "in a single forward pass with zero pre-verdict reasoning". The parser (analysis/infra/parsing.py, parse_verdict_strict) accepts a VERDICT line anywhere in the reply, Haiku's no-query verdict replies average 777 tokens, and no one has checked whether Haiku actually wrote the verdict line first. Use B4's wording, not the "verdict before the written reasoning" wording.
Member D, finding 1(b): UNSURE: If the format was followed, the visible REASONING comes after the VERDICT line for every judge, not only Haiku. The draft already says these accounts "may not be what drove the verdicts". Adding "written after the verdict line" is fine only once someone checks that judges actually followed that order.
Member D, finding 1(c): UNSURE: Long thinking chains before a verdict would explain Qwen's cut-offs, but that is speculation and needs no action.
