## The dispute

Finding 1 of Member B (severity given: major): the size and source of the one-query rise are given only through pooled figures that one judge dominates.

Defender(s): Member B (author(s) of the finding). Challenger(s): Member A, Member C.

What is in dispute: whether the pooled "about 18% more errors" (4.1) next to "Haiku supplies about 70% of the pooled error rate" (4.3) misleads enough that the main text must add the descriptive split the finding proposes (Haiku supplies about a quarter of the rise; the other six judges went from 1.53% to 2.20%, about 44% more errors; the 1.96% base rate beside the four-judge 0.94 points), or whether the existing text already separates Haiku's share of the errors from the broad rise, so that these subgroup figures are optional post hoc emphasis. Member A has accepted one part: a short clause that Haiku supplies about a quarter of the rise. The page budget in the brief applies to anything added to the main text.

## The finding as written in round 1 (Member B)

1. [major, framing; Sections 4.1 and 4.3, and through them the title and abstract] The size and source of the one-query rise are given only through pooled figures that Haiku dominates, which invites the wrong summary.
Evidence. 4.1: "at the realised 4.34%, the rise is about 18% more errors." 4.3: "Because judges count equally, Haiku supplies about 70% of the pooled error rate" and "The rise from none to one is broad: six of the seven judges erred more with one query than with none." From Table 8, Haiku's rise is 1.36 of the 5.38 judge-points (7 x 0.77), about a quarter (29 of the 115 net verdicts). The other six judges go from a mean of 1.53% to 2.20% (196 to 282 errors), about 44% more. Haiku's own rise is about 6% of its base, so "18%" blends 6% and 44%. The registered counterpart is already in the draft: without the Claude judges the rise is 0.94 points, on a no-query rate of 1.96% (Table 8), about half again as many errors. A reader who joins "70% of the error is Haiku's" to "0.77 points" will conclude that the rise is mostly one weak judge's noise. Table 8 shows the opposite: Haiku supplies most of the errors, a quarter of the rise and two thirds of the fall from one to six (3.84 of 5.72 judge-points).
Resolution (arithmetic on printed values, to be labelled descriptive).
- 4.1, replace the clause with: "at the realised 4.34%, the rise is about 18% more errors, and proportionally more for the judges with low error (Section 4.3)."
- 4.3, replace the "broad" sentence with: "The rise from none to one is broad: six of the seven judges erred more with one query than with none, and Haiku, which supplies most of the errors, supplies only about a quarter of the rise. Across the other six judges error went from 1.53% to 2.20%, about 44% more errors (a descriptive figure)."
- 4.3, in the no-Claude paragraph write "(0.94 points over the four remaining judges, on a no-query rate of 1.96%)".

## Opening refutation(s) from round 2

Member A:

Member B, finding 1(a): AGREE: Haiku’s contribution to baseline error differs substantially from its contribution to the increase. A short clarification that it supplies about a quarter of the rise would help.
Member B, finding 1(b): REFUTE: The existing pooled 18% figure is correct, and Section 4.3 already distinguishes the broad rise from Haiku’s baseline concentration and reports the positive non-Claude contrast. Requiring several additional subgroup percentages overstates the omission and adds post hoc emphasis without correcting a false claim.

Member C:

Member B, finding 1: REFUTE: The arithmetic is informative, but the existing text already distinguishes Haiku’s share of baseline errors from the broad one-query rise and its contribution to the decline. The pooled 18% figure is correct; adding several subgroup ratios is optional context, not a necessary repair to a misleading calculation.
