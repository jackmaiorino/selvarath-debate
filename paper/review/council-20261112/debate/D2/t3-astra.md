POSITION: MAINTAIN. Cross-period headline claims should be descriptive or explicitly conditional. “Effect of offering” may remain as the estimand, with its identification assumption stated.

ARGUMENT: D’s comparison between gate failures and collection timing conflates two different problems. Gate failures change the intervention delivered: a causal claim can concern offering queries through this imperfect gate. Collection timing affects whether the cross-period contrast identifies that intervention’s effect. Calling it an effect “under this gate” does not address temporal confounding. Moreover, 11–33% is exposure to flagged queries, not measured distortion of verdicts, so that percentage cannot establish which inferential problem deserves stronger wording.

D’s calculation also does not refute the Haiku counterexample. The observed +1.36-point rise combines any period change and any intervention effect; it cannot bound either component separately. A hypothetical post-period no-query error of 28.21%, followed by the observed 22.57% with one query, gives exactly the +7-point period change and −5.64-point intervention effect D calculates. Those are compatible probabilities, although the shift is substantial and there is no evidence it happened. Removing Claude establishes the observed rise in the remaining panel; it cannot constrain Haiku’s unobserved counterfactual. Thus reversal of the pooled effect does not logically require degradation across all three providers. The cross-provider pattern strengthens the treatment interpretation without identifying it.

Finally, “error was higher with one query” accurately describes an assigned intervention’s observed result. It does not imply that budgets were endogenous covariates. Assignment belongs in Methods; descriptive headlines need not encode a different study design. I defend this wording because the condition supporting the causal sign is untested and treatment collection was systematically separated in time.

IF CONCEDING: Not applicable.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT: Contemporaneous, interleaved controls would address the empirical ambiguity. With the experiment frozen, the reporting dispute is resolved by the agreed stability qualification plus descriptive or explicitly conditional cross-period headlines and caption. Within-query contrasts can retain intervention wording under their corresponding assumptions.
