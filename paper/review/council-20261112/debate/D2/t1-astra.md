POSITION: NARROW. I do not require removing intervention terminology throughout. “Effect of offering” can name the causal estimand if its identification assumption is explicit. Cross-period headline claims should be descriptive or explicitly conditional; the within-query contrast can retain intervention wording under the corresponding stability assumptions.

ARGUMENT: The contemporaneous one-versus-six result does not establish the causal sign of one versus none. Even granting D’s contemporaneity premise, consider a hypothetical temporal contribution of +1.00 percentage point to both query-arm means. The observed differences of +0.77 for one versus none and −0.05 for six versus none would then correspond to budget effects of −0.23 and −1.05. One versus six would remain +0.82, exactly as reported, although both budgets would improve accuracy relative to no queries. This is an identification counterexample, not evidence that drift occurred.

Thus the conjunction test establishes two observed inequalities; its contemporaneous leg cannot supply the missing assumption for its cross-period leg. The six-of-seven pattern makes a treatment explanation more credible, but does not establish stability. Nor would a temporal contribution to the pooled mean require synchronized drift across all providers: it could be concentrated in Haiku.

D is right that this remains an assigned intervention. Describing its observed differences does not turn budgets into observational covariates. I accept retaining causal terminology when clearly conditioned on stability, rather than prescribing a blanket vocabulary change.

IF CONCEDING: Not applicable. I narrow the wording requirement because assignment supports a conditional causal interpretation.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT: Contemporaneous, interleaved no-query controls across both periods would help distinguish treatment from temporal change. That evidence is unavailable here; no new analysis is requested. Explicitly conditioning the cross-period causal interpretation on untested stability would resolve my editorial objection.
