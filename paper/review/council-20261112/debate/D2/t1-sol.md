POSITION: NARROW. I defend descriptive wording for the cross-period result headings and Figure 2 caption. Section 3.5 may retain “effect of offering” if explicitly conditional on stable judge behaviour; causal wording need not disappear throughout.

ARGUMENT: Even granting that one versus six queries is fully contemporaneous, that comparison cannot establish that offering one query harms accuracy. Consider an illustrative upward period shift of 0.90 percentage points. The contemporaneous no-query rate would then be 5.24%, against the observed 5.11% with one query and 4.29% with six. One query would help slightly, six would help more, and the reported cross-period comparisons would still display the apparent peak. This is an identification counterexample, requiring no claim that drift actually occurred.

Thus D’s within-period comparison supports an ordering between query budgets while leaving the direction of the one-versus-none intervention effect dependent on stability. Six upward estimates across several providers strengthen the empirical pattern, but supply no contemporaneous no-query control. The headings “raised pooled error” and “Reading the evidence beat querying it” assert precisely the cross-period effects that remain conditional. Descriptive headings preserve every observed result without characterising the study as observational.

IF CONCEDING: D’s point that this was an assigned, paired intervention persuades me that blanket removal of intervention language would be excessive. Explicitly conditional causal language is appropriate.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT: Counterbalanced, contemporaneous collection of query and non-query conditions would address the temporal confounding. With this experiment frozen, I would resolve the reporting dispute through the agreed stability qualification plus descriptive cross-period headlines, while retaining qualified intervention language in the methods.
