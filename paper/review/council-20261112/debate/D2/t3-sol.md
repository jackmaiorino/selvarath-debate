POSITION: MAINTAIN. I defend descriptive cross-period headlines, while allowing explicitly conditional causal language in the methods.

ARGUMENT: D’s claim that overturning the one-query increase requires degradation across all three providers does not follow from the exclusion analysis. Consider hypothetical additive period shifts of +3, +2 and +1 percentage points for Luna, Terra and Sol, respectively, and zero for every other judge. These are compatible with every reported query-arm rate. Their pooled contribution is 6/7 = 0.86 points, enough to reverse the observed +0.77-point difference. Without the Claude judges, their contribution becomes 6/4 = 1.50 points, also reversing that panel’s +0.94-point difference. Neither Anthropic nor Qwen needs to drift. These shifts are illustrative, not evidence of drift; their plausibility is debatable, but the exclusion result does not establish the claimed necessity.

The gate analogy also conflates different inferential problems. Rule-breaking admissions are part of the implemented intervention. They limit extrapolation from this gate to verification that obeys the intended rule. Collection period, by contrast, is a possible competing cause of differences even for the implemented intervention. Moreover, an 11–33% exposure rate is not an estimate of verdict distortion, so comparing its magnitude with an unmeasured period effect cannot rank the threats.

Finally, “error was higher with” accurately describes an assigned experiment’s observed outcomes. It does not imply that budgets were endogenous covariates. The methods establish assignment; the headline need not assert an unconditional causal interpretation to distinguish this experiment from Appendix C’s observational grouping.
