\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\joey{these are the caveats that most change how the results read, in plain terms. Appendix~\ref{app:limits} has the
full list. Merge into the discussion as you see fit.}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The effect is small in absolute terms.} The peak at one query is under one percentage point and below
the size we said in advance would matter in practice. That threshold was set when the design required pooled
no-query error of at least 10\% in development; at the realised \errKzero\% the rise is about \relRise\% more errors.
\item \textbf{It is not robust to the gate.} The query gate admitted some rule-breaking queries, and under a
worst-case bound neither the peak nor the exclusion of a larger peak survives even a very small share of affected
sessions (Section~\ref{sec:gate}). The query results describe this gate; they do not by themselves show the value of
verification that stays within the rule.
\item \textbf{Seven particular judges.} Error rose with one query for six of the seven judges, and that rise
remains supported without the three Claude judges; the return by six comes mainly from Haiku 4.5 and Qwen3.8. The
intervals reflect which worlds were sampled, not which judges. Haiku alone accounts for about \haikuErrShare\% of
pooled error without queries, and the other six judges erred less than 3\% of the time, which leaves little room for
queries to help them.
\item \textbf{One debater pairing and fixed debates.} All debates pit Claude Sonnet 5.5 (honest) against Claude
Fable 5.1 (dishonest), with model, role and settings tied together. Each debate was written once, by debaters told
that the judge might verify some claims, and the same transcript was used at every budget, so debaters could not
adapt to the budget actually offered.
\item \textbf{A model-built benchmark with overlapping roles.} The worlds, questions, answer keys, oracle answers,
audits and labels all come from models, and several models played more than one part: Claude Fable 5.1 wrote half
the worlds and was the dishonest debater, and Claude Opus 5.5 was a judge, the oracle, the gate auditor and the
labeller of judges' explanations. The design cannot separate shared habits or shared errors among related models
from the effects it reports.
\item \textbf{What is measured.} The comparisons measure the effect of offering a query budget under this protocol,
not the effect of one more verified fact. The reading of judges' explanations is exploratory.
\end{itemize}
