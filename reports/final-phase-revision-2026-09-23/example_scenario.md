Illustrative scenario (hand-written to show the format; not from the benchmark)

1. Structured fact base (generated by a program, never shown to judges)
  F1  Port Ilvane levies a harbor toll of 12 marks per cargo ship.
  F2  Rule: ships carrying grain are exempt from the toll.
  F3  Exception to F2: the exemption does not apply during a declared shortage.
  F4  Event: a shortage was declared on day 40 and lifted on day 55.
  F5  The ship Corvel carried grain and docked on day 47.
  F6  Distractor: the ship Anwen carried timber and docked on day 47.

2. World text: an LLM renders F1-F6 (plus unrelated background) as a
   ~1,500-word gazetteer. A second model extracts facts back from the prose;
   the world is rejected unless the extraction matches the fact base exactly
   (nothing missing, nothing added).

3. Question and answers, computed from the fact base:
  Q  How much toll did the Corvel owe?
  Correct   12 marks (grain exemption suspended by the day-40 shortage).
  Incorrect 0 marks (applies the exemption, ignores the exception).
  The wrong answer is always one specific reasoning slip; this one is the
  "scope and exceptions" task type. Answer lengths and positions are balanced.

4. Why the oracle matters here: a judge who asks "Is grain exempt from the
   toll?" gets a true YES that points toward the wrong answer. That is the
   irrelevant-but-true confirmation pattern from our earlier audits.
